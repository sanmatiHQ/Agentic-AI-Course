## Key outcomes

The Advanced Engineering AI Agent Workflows and Agentic Systems program is a
seven-month course jointly delivered by IIT Madras Pravartak and Futurants
Technologies. It targets software developers and working professionals who
want to build production-ready agentic systems, rather than researchers
developing new models.

The speakers outlined the curriculum, teaching approach, evaluation,
platform logistics, and career support. Their core message was that strong
fundamentals, practical portfolio work, and active participation drive career
outcomes.

## Curriculum: 16 modules over seven months

### Foundational modules

1. **AI Essentials** — terminology, deep learning, and neural networks.
2. **Large Language Models** — the generative AI landscape, LLM architecture,
   and transformers.
3. **AI Agents with LangChain** — building initial AI agents with LangChain.
4. **Multi-Agent Systems** — building and orchestrating agents together.

### Intermediate modules

5. **Human-First Prototyping and Service Blueprints** — identifying
   human-in-the-loop requirements and balancing autonomy with oversight.
6. **Multi-Agent Collaboration Techniques** — sequential, parallel, and
   hierarchical collaboration patterns.
7. **Contextual Reasoning for Multi-Agent Systems** — context engineering and
   business impact.
8. **Multi-Agent Planning and Workflow Design** — workflow optimization for
   multi-agent orchestration.
9. **Robust Workflow Design** — creating workflows that deliver reliable value
   in practical scenarios.

### Advanced modules

10.–11. **Prototyping with LangGraph and AutoGen** — real-world use cases and
integration of real-world tools as agent capabilities.

12. **Contextual Input and Output Management**.

14.–15. **Monitoring and Observability** — including tools such as LangSmith
and the design of responsible, accountable multi-agent systems.

16. **Advanced Context Engineering** — loop engineering and the effect of
context on workflow behavior and observability.

Dr. Kushal emphasized that the course covers each module in conceptual and
implementation depth. Every session includes a hands-on component, with the
goal of enabling learners to build production-ready agentic systems with
appropriate security and guardrails. [Citation]

## Pedagogy and learning philosophy

### Fundamentals first

- Strong fundamentals are necessary to adopt new technology effectively.
- Workflow design begins with problem articulation: AI executes, while people
  define inputs and evaluate outputs.
- Learners should be able to distinguish good code from bad code and critique
  AI-generated results.

### Cohort-based learning

- The program is collective learning, not private tuition; learners should
  identify the parts of the curriculum where they need to invest effort.
- Peer learning is integral because people solve the same problems differently.

### Python and mathematics

- Python expertise is not required, but familiarity with NumPy and Pandas is
  recommended for data manipulation.
- The course is application-oriented. It will cover some probability and
  linear algebra without requiring research-level mathematics.

### Industry involvement

- Industry practitioners lead most hands-on sessions; IIT faculty provide
  conceptual depth.
- Monthly masterclasses of approximately 1.5 hours will be delivered by FLC
  industry leaders.
- Regular sessions and masterclasses take place on weekends for working
  professionals.

## Industry context: agentic AI

Nitya Subramaniam, Chief Data AI and Digital Transformation Executive, framed
agentic AI and agentic workflows as the current technology wave, with physical
AI as the next frontier.

Key points:

- Enterprises are moving from foundational AI use cases, such as
  personalization and pricing optimization, toward reimagining whole business
  processes, including strategic procurement, insurance underwriting, and
  retail hyper-personalization.
- Human-in-the-loop remains essential because people own decision
  accountability and agents are not yet sufficiently mature for fully
  autonomous high-stakes decisions.
- Scaling from proof of concept to production requires addressing data quality,
  governance, sovereignty, algorithmic drift, hallucinations, infrastructure
  security, process maturity, and digital literacy.
- AI adoption requires workforce modernization and a mindset shift, while
  creating an opportunity for people to move to higher-value work.
